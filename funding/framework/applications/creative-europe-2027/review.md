That is 30/100 in total. The draft is about 6,000 characters against a Part B of roughly 70 pages. It reads as a skeleton, not a submission.

### 2. Unsupported or inflated claims

- **"542,000 registered players" [F-001]:** unverified, and it counts web, Telegram and apps together. Much of it probably comes from the SEI on-chain campaign era (see F-003 to F-006), but the draft calls these people "players" without saying so. The verified Android figure is only 1K+ downloads (F-016).
- **"186,000 followers on X" [F-011]:** unverified. It sits next to 4,700 Instagram followers (F-012), and a 40:1 ratio will look like airdrop or bot inflation to a panel.
- **"peak of 307,600 monthly web visitors" [F-002]:** unverified. It is a peak, not current traffic, and it dates from 2025.
- **"800+ strays saved" [F-024]:** a number hardcoded on our own homepage, unverified, with no shelter confirming it. It should not appear in an EU dossier.
- **"characters that European players already recognise from our live game":** no fact base entry supports this.
- **"a genre that European studios lead":** no source given.
- **"two contracted roles that the panel has asked for in winning dossiers":** unsupported, and presumptuous toward the evaluators. Delete it.
- **"a named narrative lead":** no name is given.
- **"An external studio performs playtesting" / "a contracted artist":** neither is named, and there is no letter of intent (LOI).
- **"localisation into five EU languages is budgeted":** the languages aren't listed, and the budget annex isn't visible.
- **"the same shelter partners that feature in the story":** no shelters are named anywhere.
- **"Our studio has already shipped five game modes" [F-018]:** true, but these are free casual modes inside a Web3 product. That is not the "previously released game with a sales report" the call requires.
- **"we know how to take a game through store review and live operations":** the only store evidence is one iOS listing and 1K+ Android downloads.
- **"our own share comes from revenue and private funding":** filed FY2025 revenue is €105 (F-022). Nothing in the fact base supports the private funding.

### 3. Token promotion, speculation or gambling

- The prose leaves out crypto, but every traction number it cites belongs to a blockchain product. That product runs Stellar Soroban NFTs, a custodial wallet per user, and XLM/USDC payments (F-020, F-025), and has an SEI on-chain history (F-003 to F-006).
- A panel that opens the website or the App Store listing will find NFTs and "Blessing" and "Pass" contracts within minutes. Leaving this out reads as concealment, which is worse than disclosing it.
- The 2026 exclusion on gambling mechanics creates a risk here. If Blessings or Passes are paid, randomised or tradeable, the panel may treat the live product, and the track record built on it, as gambling-adjacent.
- The dossier should state plainly that the new game has no blockchain, no NFTs and no tradeable items.
- "Every chapter is also a campaign for real adoptions" plus "turns a single-player narrative into a continuing relationship" hints at live-service engagement loops. The draft doesn't define it, so it invites monetisation questions.

### 4. Missing annexes and evidence

**Eligibility gates (these block the application before scoring):**

- The applicant is an MB with NACE 58.21 (F-021). The call needs an independent production company with a non-publishing NACE code, and there is no evidence of the UAB conversion.
- There is no previously released game with a sales report. €105 of revenue does not meet the track-record requirement.

**Missing documents:**

- **Financial capacity:** balance sheet and profit-and-loss statement, plus proof of the 40% co-financing (bank statements or investor commitments).
- **Budget:** budget table and budget summary. Neither was provided, and the prose defers to them.
- **Team:** CVs and named contracts or LOIs for the narrative lead, the artist, the playtesting and accessibility studio, music and localisation.
- **Partners:** LOIs from European publishers and from the partner shelters.
- **Rights:** chain-of-title and rights documentation, including releases from shelters for the real cat case files. The draft also doesn't say whether the backstories of the abandoning owners involve personal data.
- **The game itself:** a game design document summary, hand-made key art and concept samples, and a pitch video or prototype.
- **Market:** market analysis, competitor titles, pricing and platform strategy.
- **Work plan:** a Gantt chart with months, durations, deliverables and milestones.
- **AI use:** a declaration covering the OpenAI and Gemini pipeline (F-019).

### 5. The three changes that would raise the score most

1. **Fix eligibility and track record first.** Complete the UAB conversion with a non-publishing NACE code. Then find a released, sold title with a real sales report, for example by partnering with a co-producer who has one. Without this, nothing else matters.
2. **Name people and partners and attach proof.** That means the narrative lead, the artist, the QA studio, two or three European publishers with LOIs, and named shelters in named cities with signed partnership letters and case-file rights. This alone could lift C2, C5 and C6 by 10 to 15 points.
3. **Write the actual game and plan.** Describe the core loop, the chapter count and runtime, the platforms, the art direction with samples, and a month-by-month work plan with deliverables. Also:
   - Replace the inflated reach numbers with verified, non-crypto figures.
   - Disclose the Web3 history in one clear sentence and state that the new title has no tokens.
   - Drop the AI pipeline from the "innovation" section.

### 6. Verdict

**REJECT.** The applicant fails both eligibility gates: the entity type and NACE code, and the lack of a released game with a sales report. On the merits it scores about 30/100. Claims are unsupported and unnamed, the timeline is empty, co-financing is not credible, and traction borrowed from a blockchain product will not survive a panel's first look at the website.
