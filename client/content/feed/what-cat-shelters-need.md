Many cat shelters say money helps most, because it pays for vet care, sterilisation and whatever runs out that week. After that, they need the food and litter they already use, cleaning supplies, and people's time. The best first step is always the same: ask the shelter what it needs right now.

Here is what usually tops the list, and how to give in a way that helps rather than adds work.

<!-- key-takeaways -->
- Money is the most flexible gift: it covers vet bills and sterilisation, which goods cannot.
- Match supplies to the shelter's own wish list; many accept only unopened food.
- Cleaning supplies, litter and laundry items run out constantly.
- Time counts: volunteering and fostering free up staff.
- You can give to a shelter in another country online, and on some platforms check the payout yourself.

## Money vs goods: what helps most and why

Goods feel more personal, but money is usually the bigger help:

- **It goes where the need is.** One week it is a vet bill, the next it is heating or a broken washing machine. Only cash covers all of those.
- **It pays for vet care and sterilisation.** These are often a shelter's biggest costs, and you cannot donate a surgery in a bag.
- **It buys the right thing.** Shelters know which food suits a kitten, a senior cat or a cat on a kidney diet. They often get better prices buying in bulk.
- **It saves time.** Every donated bag has to be sorted, checked and stored. Money needs none of that.

The ASPCA puts both on its list of ways to help: shelters "always appreciate donations of vital supplies," and funds "can make a world of difference for your local shelter" ([ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/want-help-animals-new-year-here-are-5-ways-you-can-make-difference)).

## The usual cat shelter wish list

If you want to give goods, these are the items shelters most often ask for. Check with the shelter first; brands and types matter.

**Food**
- Unopened dry and wet cat food, ideally the brand the shelter already feeds (a sudden change upsets cats' stomachs).
- Kitten food and kitten milk replacer, in spring and summer especially.
- Special diets only if the shelter asks for them.

**Litter and hygiene**
- Cat litter of the type the shelter uses (clumping, wood pellets or silica: ask).
- Litter trays and scoops.
- Puppy pads or newspaper for kittens and sick cats.

**Cleaning**
- Bleach or the disinfectant the shelter uses, washing powder, bin bags, paper towels, disposable gloves.

**Comfort**
- Clean towels and fleece blankets in good condition.
- Scratching posts, simple toys, carriers and cat beds.

The ASPCA's advice is simple: "Call ahead or check their social media to see what items are most in demand" ([ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/want-help-animals-new-year-here-are-5-ways-you-can-make-difference)). Many shelters post a wish list or an online shop list you can buy from directly.

## Vet care and sterilisation

Vet care is where much of a shelter's money goes. Cats arrive with injuries, infections, parasites or no vaccinations, and most need sterilising before they can be rehomed or returned outside.

Sterilisation also matters beyond the shelter's walls. Trap-neuter-return with ongoing monitoring (TNRM), which sterilises, vaccinates and returns outdoor cats and then keeps an eye on the colony, is described by the ASPCA as "the most humane, efficient way of stabilizing community cat populations" ([ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/helping-shelters-people-pets/closer-look-community-cats)). If preventing future strays matters to you, ask the shelter whether sterilisation or TNR is part of its work.

## Time: volunteering and fostering

Not every gift is money or goods.

- **Volunteering:** cleaning, feeding, laundry, socialising shy cats, driving cats to the vet, taking good photos for rehoming posts, or helping with a fundraiser.
- **Fostering:** a foster home gives a cat, a mother with kittens or a cat recovering from surgery a quiet place outside the shelter, and frees a space for the next one.
- **Skills:** design, translation, accounting, social media or repairs can save a small shelter real money.

Looking to give a shelter cat a home? Contact your local shelter; they run their own rehoming.
