To sponsor a cat online, you sign up on a shelter's or charity's website for a regular gift (usually monthly) toward its cat care, and in return you get updates on a cat or a pen of cats. Sponsorship does not change where the cat lives: the shelter keeps caring for it and keeps looking for its new home.

Below: how two well-known schemes describe their own sponsorship, the questions worth asking before you sign up, and smaller one-off ways to help a shelter if a monthly plan is not for you.

<!-- key-takeaways -->
- Sponsorship is a gift toward care, not ownership. The cat stays with the shelter until it finds a home.
- Big charities often have you sponsor a **pen**: when one cat is rehomed, the next cat moves in.
- Before you sign up, ask what the money covers, how often you hear back, and how to stop.
- If you prefer one-off gifts, you can give $1 at a time to Pink Paw, a small shelter in Kaunas, and check each payout on a public page.

## How cat sponsorship works

Most sponsorship schemes share the same shape: a regular gift, a welcome pack, and news about the cats your money supports. The details differ, so here is how two large UK charities explain theirs, in their own words (checked on 20 October 2026).

**Cats Protection** sponsors sign up for a pen, not one cat. Its FAQ explains that you sponsor a pen at one of its centres, "So whenever we rehome a cat, a new cat will take their place." Sponsors get updates about the cats in their pen, including through an online portal, and the page says sponsorship starts "from just £6 a month". Sponsors are welcome to visit a centre if they call first.

**Woodgreen** works in a similar way. Its page says sponsorship supports "lots of homeless pets, one after the other": when one finds a home, another comes in. It lists monthly email updates, three postal updates a year, a welcome pack and a certificate, starting from £6.50 a month.

Why a pen and not one cat? Because the goal of a shelter is for cats to leave. A scheme tied to one cat would end the day that cat finds a family. A pen keeps your gift useful for as long as you give.

## What your sponsorship pays for

Each charity says this in its own words, and it is worth reading them rather than guessing:

- Cats Protection says sponsorship "helps fund the food, shelter and medical care of every cat that stays in your pen".
- Woodgreen lists a safe, warm shelter, nutritious food, medical checks and life-saving operations, plus toys, games and behaviour training to help pets find the right home.

In other words, sponsorship money usually goes into the general cost of caring for the cats, not into a separate pot for one animal. That is a good thing: a shelter can then spend it where it is needed that week, whether that is a vet bill or cat litter. Our guide to [what cat shelters need](/feed/cats/what-cat-shelters-need) goes into the everyday costs.

## Questions to ask before you sign up

A good scheme answers these on its website or by email:

1. **What exactly does my gift cover?** Look for the charity's own wording, as above.
2. **Do I sponsor a cat, a pen or a centre?** And can I choose which one?
3. **How often will I hear back?** Monthly emails, a few letters a year, or an online portal?
4. **How do I change or cancel it?** Monthly gifts should be easy to stop.
5. **Is the charity registered?** In England and Wales you can look it up on the [Charity Commission register](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/). For other checks, read [how to tell if an animal rescue is legit](/feed/giving/how-to-tell-if-an-animal-rescue-is-legit).

If a scheme promises that your money pays for "your" cat alone, ask how that works when the cat leaves. Honest schemes are clear about it.

## Sponsoring a cat at a small shelter abroad

Big charities have sponsorship teams and welcome packs. Small shelters usually do not. If you want to help a local shelter in another country, the simplest route is often a one-off or repeat donation on its own website. Our guide to [donating to a cat shelter online](/feed/giving/donate-to-a-cat-shelter-online) covers ways to do that without large fees.

When there is no sponsorship scheme, you can make your own routine: put a reminder in your calendar and give the same small amount every month.
