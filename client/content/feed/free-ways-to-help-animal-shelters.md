You can help an animal shelter for free by sharing its animals, giving your time or skills, fostering, collecting the goods it asks for, and using free tools where a sponsor pays for your tap. Some of these move real money to the shelter; most of them help in other ways, and that is just as useful.

Here are twelve, with an honest note on which is which.

<!-- key-takeaways -->
- Much of the most useful free help is people: foster homes, volunteers and shares that reach the right home.
- Always ask for the shelter's current wish list before you collect goods.
- Some "free" actions move money because a sponsor pays. Check who the sponsor is, what the cap is, and whether the payout is public.
- On the Token Tails [give page](/shelter-payouts/give), one tap and Token Tails sends Pink Paw a free 0.01 USDC treat from its own budget.

## Which free actions actually move money?

| Free action | Moves money to the shelter? | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Sharing animals and appeals | No | Finds homes, fosters and donors |
| Volunteering (in person or online) | No | Gives staff time back |
| Fostering | No | Frees a shelter space; the shelter or rescue usually covers the costs |
| Collecting wish-list goods | Indirectly | Saves the shelter buying them |
| Tax-share schemes (where they exist) | Yes | Part of tax you already pay goes to the shelter |
| Sponsored taps and game treats | Yes, small amounts | A named sponsor pays for each tap |
| Reviews and word of mouth | No | Builds trust with future visitors and givers |

## 1. Share the shelter's animals the right way

A share is the cheapest help there is, and it works best when it points to the source. Share the shelter's own post or page, not a screenshot, so people land where they can actually ask about an animal. Add one line of your own ("Shy but sweet, good with other cats") and it travels further.

## 2. Volunteer your skills online

Many shelters need help they can get from anyone with a laptop: editing photos, writing animal profiles, running social media, translating, or keeping spreadsheets. Best Friends Animal Society, for example, describes a remote volunteer who collected and summarised studies for its education work from home ([Best Friends](https://bestfriends.org/stories/features/virtual-volunteering)). Email your local shelter and offer one specific skill; it is easier to say yes to than "anything".

## 3. Volunteer in person

Cleaning, laundry, socialising shy cats, walking dogs and driving animals to the vet all take hours that staff rarely have. Expect an induction and a regular slot: reliability matters more than enthusiasm.

## 4. Foster

Fostering takes an animal out of the shelter and into your home for a while, which frees a space for the next one. The shelter or rescue usually provides food and supplies and covers vet care ([ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/what-does-it-mean-be-animal-foster-caregiver); [RSPCA](https://www.rspca.org.uk/findapet/foster/cat)). Our guide on [how to foster a cat](/feed/cats/how-to-foster-a-cat) walks through what to expect.

## 5. Ask for the wish list before you give goods

Unrequested items can cost a shelter time to sort and store. Ask first, then collect what is on the list: often cleaning supplies, litter and a specific food. See [what cat shelters need](/feed/cats/what-cat-shelters-need) for the usual asks and what to avoid.

## 6. Run a collection

You do not have to buy anything yourself. A box at work, school or a birthday party, with the shelter's list taped on top, turns other people's spare items into exactly what the shelter needs.

## 7. Use a tax-share scheme if your country has one

Some countries let you send part of the income tax you already pay to a registered organisation, at no cost to you. In Lithuania, for example, people can assign 1.2% of their income tax to a non-profit; Pink Paw explains how on its [support page](https://rozinepedute.lt/parama/). Check what your country offers.

## 8. Leave a review and tell your friends

A short, honest review on Google or Facebook helps the next person decide to visit, foster or give. Mention what you saw: clean rooms, staff who knew each animal by name, honest answers.

## 9. Help strays the right way

If you see an injured or clearly lost cat, contact your local shelter or rescue instead of waiting for someone else to. Our guide on [how to help stray cats](/feed/cats/how-to-help-stray-cats) covers what to do, and when to leave a healthy community cat alone.

## 12. Check appeals before you share them

Fake rescue appeals spread through the same shares real ones do. Before you pass one on, check that the rescue is registered, reachable and consistent across its own channels. Our checklist on [how to tell if an animal rescue is legit](/feed/giving/how-to-tell-if-an-animal-rescue-is-legit) walks through the checks.

## How to check a free action is real

Any "click to help" offer should pass three tests:

1. **The sponsor is named.** Someone has to pay. If a page cannot say who, assume nobody does.
2. **The amount and the cap are public.** "We donate for every click" means little without a value per click and a daily limit.
3. **The payout is checkable.** You should be able to see what was actually paid, to whom and when: a published report, a receipt, or a public record like a [verifiable on-chain payout](/feed/giving/verify-crypto-donation).
