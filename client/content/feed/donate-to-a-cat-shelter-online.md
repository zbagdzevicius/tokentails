To donate to a cat shelter online, check that the shelter is real, then give through the method that leaves it the most money: usually a bank transfer, otherwise card or PayPal through its own website. If you want proof that the money arrived, choose a way to give that leaves a public receipt, such as an on-chain payout you can look up yourself.

This guide works for any shelter, near or far. We use Pink Paw, a cat shelter in Kaunas, Lithuania, as the worked example, because it is the shelter we work with and we can show every step.

<!-- key-takeaways -->
- Check the shelter in its country's official register and on its own channels before you give.
- A bank transfer usually leaves the shelter the most; inside the EU, euro transfers abroad cost the same as at home.
- Card and PayPal are fastest, but fees come out of the gift.
- Food, litter and wish-list items help as much as money if the shelter asks for them.
- On-chain payouts give you a public receipt: see Pink Paw's at [/shelter-payouts](/shelter-payouts).

## Step 1: pick a shelter you can verify

A good shelter is easy to check. Before you give, look for four things:

1. **Registration.** Search the official register for its country. In the US that is the [IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search); in England and Wales the [Charity Commission register](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/); in Lithuania the Registrų centras legal entities register.
2. **A real address and contact details** that match across its website and social accounts.
3. **Recent photos and updates.** Active shelters post their cats, their needs and their events every week or so.
4. **Published numbers.** Many shelters publish yearly intake and rehoming figures or annual reports. Pink Paw, for example, keeps a [statistics page](https://rozinepedute.lt/statistika/).

Be careful with fundraisers that only exist as a single social post or a crowdfunding page with no organisation behind it. If you cannot find who runs it, give elsewhere.

## Step 2: choose how to give

| Method | Speed | What reaches the shelter | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank transfer | Same day to 2 days | Usually all of it | Cheapest in the shelter's currency |
| Card on the shelter's site | Instant | Gift minus card fees | Easiest for one-off gifts |
| PayPal | Instant | Gift minus PayPal fees | Works across borders |
| Wish list or goods | Days | Exactly what was asked for | Check the list is current |
| Tax-share schemes | Yearly | A share of tax you already pay | Country-specific, e.g. Lithuania's 1.2% |
| Stablecoins (USDC) | Seconds to minutes | The full gift; the sender pays a small network fee on top | Public, checkable receipt |

Monthly gifts help shelters plan, even small ones. A regular gift lets a shelter budget for food and vet bills instead of reacting to emergencies.

## Giving to a cat shelter abroad

Giving across borders is easy now, but small costs add up.

- **Currency.** Give in the shelter's currency when you can. If your bank converts for you, compare its rate with the market rate.
- **Bank transfers in the EU.** EU rules say a bank must charge the same for a euro payment to another EU country as for an equivalent domestic one, [as Your Europe explains](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/financial-products-and-services/payments-transfers-cheques/index_en.htm). For a shelter in Lithuania, a euro transfer from Germany or France should cost what a local transfer costs.
- **PayPal and cards** are convenient from outside the EU, but fees come out of the gift.
- **Tax relief.** It usually applies only to charities registered in your own country. Check your local rules if that matters to you.

**Example: Pink Paw, Kaunas.** Pink Paw's [support page](https://rozinepedute.lt/parama/) lists a bank transfer to its Swedbank account, PayPal and Contribee. People who pay Lithuanian income tax can also assign 1.2% of it to the shelter at no cost to themselves.

## Small ways to help that are not money

Shelters need people as much as payments:

- **Share** a shelter's posts about cats looking for homes. One share can reach the right person.
- **Foster** a cat for a few weeks if a shelter near you runs a foster programme.
- **Volunteer** for cleaning, socialising shy cats, transport or photography.
- **Donate goods** from the shelter's current list: food, litter and cleaning supplies are common asks.
- **Report** a lost or injured cat to a local shelter or rescue instead of waiting.
